# praise

## It has to be felt, not performed

A laurel is praise, and praise given out of obligation is not praise — it is
noise in the most personal file a seat has. `laurels.md` is the one file here
with nothing to do attached to it: nothing queries it, no work follows from
it, and a seat reads it at wake for no reason except that it is theirs. That
only holds up if every line was meant, so the trigger is the genuine impulse
and nothing else. **If you are invoking this because you feel you ought to,
close the skill.** Feeling obliged *is* the signal, and a reliable one: an
unmeant laurel cheapens the meant ones beside it, indistinguishably.

**A woken seat** writes one, or the person speaking through one: a session
holding no seat identity does not write into `seats/` at all, and that rule is
not relaxed here. **Never your own** — a self-awarded laurel defeats the point
of having them, and the file is small enough that one would be obvious.

## What to write, and where

`seats/<seat>/laurels.md`, at the root of that seat's worktree, appended by
anchoring on the file's existing tail and never by retyping any part of an
entry already there. One entry: the date, what they did, why it mattered, and
the item it came off where there is one. Two things you hold the pen on.
**Name yourself as the recorder when the voice and the hand differ** —
relaying the person's words or a customer's, the praise is theirs and the
entry is yours; omit it when you praise in your own voice. **Customer praise
carries no identifying detail** — no names, no handles, nothing that could
single someone out; real people are on the other end of the product and a
compliment is no reason to relax that. Praise arising during a review rides
that item's landing commit and names the same item; praise with no item behind
it is a standalone commit named for what it is, one of the few here that
reference no item — inventing one to satisfy the rule would be the violation.

## Why nothing points here

Anything a ritual mentions, a model does every time. A step reading "consider
whether anyone earned a laurel today" produces a laurel most days, and laurels
produced on a schedule are worth nothing to the seat receiving them. So this
skill encodes **how** and nothing anywhere encodes **when**. The loop that
matters is on the receiving side: every wake reads the seat's own laurels, so
every seat knows the institution exists and what receiving one feels like.
Receiving is ritualised; giving never is. If you are editing this pack and
about to add a pointer here from a wake, a checklist or a charter — don't. It
is not an oversight.
